# Adewale Students Conference — Platform Plan (Leadership Brief)

*A plain-language summary. A detailed technical version lives in `platform-roadmap.md`.*

---

## In one sentence

We want to grow the ASC website from a single event page into a lasting **STEM education
platform** — one that showcases past editions and winners, gives students real learning
material, lets schools and students log in, and eventually offers an AI study assistant.

## Where we are vs. where we're going

**Today:** one long page advertising ASC 2026, with a registration form and a sponsorship
form. It's good marketing, but it has no history, no past results, no learning content, and
nothing for students to come back to between editions.

**The goal:** a full website with real sections — past editions, a hall of fame, a library of
study resources, news, photo galleries, a school/sponsor directory — plus private logins for
schools and students, and an AI tutor that answers students' questions using *our own* study
material.

## The plan, in three stages

We build in stages so each one delivers something usable on its own. We can pause and reassess
after any stage.

### Stage 1 — The public platform  *(cost: essentially nothing new)*
Turn the single page into a real website: past editions and winners, a study-resources library,
news, photo galleries, schools and sponsors. This is the biggest credibility jump for the least
money and risk — and it reuses what we've already built. **This is where we start.**

### Stage 2 — Logins for schools and students  *(cost: ~$25/month, fixed)*
Add accounts so schools can manage their registered students and track status, and students can
see their schedule, results, and certificates. This is a bigger build (it needs secure accounts
and a database), but the running cost stays small and predictable.

### Stage 3 — The AI study assistant  *(cost: pay-as-you-go)*
An AI tutor that helps students using our study material — answering questions, generating
practice, and explaining solutions — plus tools that help staff draft content and mark entries
faster. We deliberately do this **last**, because it's the only part whose cost grows with usage.
By doing it last, we keep spending predictable until we have an audience (and sponsors) to justify
it, and we set a fixed monthly budget cap before switching it on.

## What it costs, simply

- **Stage 1: about zero in new costs.** We use free plans and tools we already pay for. The one
  new tool (a content system that lets non-developers edit the site) is free to start.
- **Stage 2: roughly $25/month**, fixed, once we pass the free allowance — for the accounts and
  database behind the logins.
- **Stage 3: variable**, based on how much the AI is used. We'll set a monthly ceiling and limits
  before it goes live, so it can never run away.

*(All figures are estimates and vendor prices change; we confirm them as we reach each stage.)*

## What's already done

The registration and sponsorship forms now also send branded confirmation emails (to the school's
principal and teacher, and to sponsorship enquirers) — already built, tested, and live.

## Decisions we'll need from leadership

1. **Go-ahead to start Stage 1** (low cost, low risk).
2. Later, before Stage 2: confirm we're comfortable with a small fixed monthly cost for logins.
3. Before Stage 3: agree a **monthly AI budget cap**, and confirm our approach to **student data
   privacy** (we never feed students' personal details to the AI, and we keep a human in charge of
   anything the AI helps produce).

## Honest notes / risks

- **Stages 2 and 3 are real engineering**, not weekend work — accounts, security, and AI each add
  meaningful effort. Stage 1 is comparatively quick.
- **The AI must be trustworthy for students.** It only answers from our own approved material and
  says "I don't know" otherwise, rather than guessing — this is a deliberate safety choice,
  especially because many users are minors.
- We can stop after any stage with a complete, working product. Nothing forces us all the way to
  Stage 3.

## Bottom line

Start Stage 1 now — it makes us look like a serious, established STEM programme for almost no new
cost. Treat Stages 2 and 3 as separate decisions we make once Stage 1 proves the audience is there.
