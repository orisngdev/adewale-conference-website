# A Student's Participation In An Edition Is Its Own Row

A Rep is **retagged** into the next edition rather than duplicated (ADR-0004), so one `students` row carries every year's history. The cost is that `students.edition_year` holds only the *latest* year: retagging overwrites the previous one, and nothing then records that the same child also took part in 2024 and 2025. `student_editions` adds one row per student per edition they took part in, carrying the registration and the school **as at that edition**, so participation is stated rather than inferred.

## Considered options

- **A `student_editions` join table (chosen).** Participation becomes a fact with a primary key, `(student_id, edition_year)`, so "who took part in 2025" is a query rather than a reconstruction, and a returning Rep's earlier years survive their retagging. It carries `registration_id`, which closes a second hole in the same place: a `students` row has no link to a registration, so a row created by hand lands on the roster with nothing behind it — ITORI COMPREHENSIVE HIGH SCHOOL sat three test Reps on the 2026 roster with candidate numbers 266–268 and no 2026 registration at all. It also carries `school_id` per edition, so a Rep who changes school between years stops rewriting their own history. Costs a table, a backfill, and a second place that must be written whenever a Rep is provisioned.
- **Keep deriving participation from `student_stage_results`.** What `20260919090000` does today: the school breakdown selects `edition_year = v_edition OR a result exists for the stage`. Rejected as the general answer because **participation precedes results**. All 605 of 2026's Reps have registered, been numbered and had sheets printed, and not one has a score; on this rule the entire live edition is invisible. It also cannot answer anything about a Rep who was entered and did not sit, which is exactly who a "did they turn up" question is about.
- **Stop retagging: one `students` row per student per edition.** Restores per-edition truth by duplicating the row, and directly contradicts ADR-0004. Rejected: it re-splits a child's identity across years, so `students.id` stops being a person and becomes a person-year — every certificate, plan assignment and access code would have to pick which one it belongs to, and the access code a Rep already signs in with would change annually.
- **An `edition_years int[]` column on `students`.** Cheapest to add. Rejected: it cannot hold the registration or the school for each year, has no referential integrity, and array containment queries fit badly with the `.in()`-and-chunk pattern every reader here already uses.

## Notes

- **The gap is small today and grows every year.** 22 students hold results in two editions, and **27 across 2022–2025 sat an edition they are no longer tagged to** (7, 6, 11, 3) — invisible to an `edition_year = Y` query. Retagging into 2026 has only touched 3 of 605 rows so far, which is why this is cheap to fix now and will not be later.
- **The backfill has two sources, and the union is the point.** Every `students` row contributes its current tag; every `student_stage_results` row contributes a year that tag may have overwritten. A student with a 2025 result who now reads 2026 gets both.
- **`registration_id` is nullable on purpose.** Pre-2026 rows cannot always be resolved to one registration — 17 historical school-editions have more than one, and The Grok Academy has two verified 2026 registrations — so the backfill takes the earliest and leaves the column null where there is no match rather than inventing a link. Nullable here means "not known", never "not required"; new writes set it.
- **This table does not become the roster on day one.** It is additive: nothing reads it until each reader is moved over deliberately, starting with the per-edition roster queries that lose retagged Reps. `students.edition_year` stays as the cheap "current edition" marker the provisioning path already relies on.
- **Not modelled:** withdrawal. A Rep replaced mid-edition keeps their participation row, because they did take part; `students.deactivated_at` remains the signal for who is on the roster now.
