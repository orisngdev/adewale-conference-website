# Attendance Is Marked By Centre Staff Who Hold No Account

Turnout at the Zonal centres is recorded on exam day by the Fellows staffing each hall — Centre Leads, Invigilators, Materials Officers — from their own phones, against a roster of `paper_exam_candidates`. They are identified by an **email an admin registered against a centre**, carried in an HMAC-signed cookie, not by a Supabase auth user. Marks land in `paper_exam_attendance` (one row per candidate per exam, latest state) with an append-only `paper_exam_attendance_events` beside it (who said so, and when). The venues themselves become rows for the first time, in `exam_centres`, because 2026 runs at ten school-named venues while `registrations.qualification_zone` still stores eight town names.

## Considered options

- **Registered emails, signed cookie, service-role reads (chosen).** Nothing to provision and nothing to revoke afterwards: an admin adds forty people from the Fellows sheet in one paste, and deactivating a row ends that person's session at their next tap, because `is_active` is re-checked on every request rather than trusted from the cookie. The new tables carry admin-only RLS and no `anon` policy at all — the lead pages reach data through the service-role key and scope themselves in application code, the same "no policy to get wrong" posture `paper_exam_items` has. The cost is stated plainly: **an email alone is the credential**, so anyone who guesses a staff address and a centre sees that centre's roster of real student names and can mark it. Mitigated only by rate limiting, the active flag, and the event log naming whoever marked. A one-column access code is the upgrade path and was deliberately deferred.
- **A magic link per lead.** Strongest of the three, and rejected on the day's logistics rather than on security: it makes marking depend on an email arriving and being opened on a phone with working data, at 6:30am, at rural centres. A register that cannot be opened is worse than one opened too easily.
- **Real auth users with a `centre_lead` role.** Would put RLS back in charge, which is where tenant scope belongs everywhere else in this codebase. Rejected for the ratio: forty accounts, forty password resets and a fourth value in the `user_role` enum, all for a single morning, plus a new permission-module key — which `getAdminPermissions` fills with `"none"` for every stored map, silently revoking access for existing admins. Attendance therefore reuses the `participants` module rather than minting one.
- **Infer attendance from whether a bubble sheet came back.** What the model does today, and what ADR-0010 names as the gap: it answers only after grading, and it answers "no sheet" identically for a candidate who never came and one whose sheet was lost. The question "did they turn up" needs asking at the time, by someone standing in the room.

## Notes

- **A centre is a row, and the mapping is explicit.** `exam_centres.legacy_zone` carries the `ZONAL_FINALS_OPTIONS` town a venue serves, so existing allocations keep working unchanged; `src/lib/forms.ts` already warned that repointing the stored values would make every past allocation read as unallocated. Eight venues claim their town and Iko Gateway claims Idiroko's, which it replaces. **Arigbajo and Imeko are new for 2026 and claim nothing** — no stored string can reach them, so schools sitting there need `registrations.exam_centre_id` set by hand, and the admin page surfaces exactly that list rather than leaving it to be discovered on the morning.
- **Allocated means allocated.** `resolveCentreId` is deliberately stricter than `examCentre()`: only an explicit allocation counts, never the school's own request and never its LGA. A lead needs the candidates sent to their hall, not the ones whose LGA happens to share a name with it. `null` is a gap to fix, not a fallback.
- **A candidate nobody allocated can still be marked.** They are searchable by name or number from any centre and marked at the centre they were actually found in — `paper_exam_attendance.centre_id` records where the mark happened, which for a walk-in is not where they were sent. They are never listed by default: a scrollable list of every unallocated candidate invites marking the wrong one.
- **Marking is per tap, not a batch save.** A lead marks over several hours as candidates arrive, so a single end-of-session submit that fails loses the morning. Each tap is its own write; a failed one leaves the row visibly unsaved with "tap again" rather than optimistically ticked, because a phantom tick is how a present candidate ends the day recorded absent.
- **"Not marked" is a state, not a missing value.** It is what a lead still has to account for, and it is not absent. The end-of-day sweep turns it into absent explicitly, in batches, and says how many it is about to change.
- **The posted student id is never a scope.** Each mark re-derives the lead from the cookie, re-reads their row, re-checks the exam is still open, and re-resolves which centre that candidate belongs to before writing — a targeted two-query lookup rather than the full sitting, because this path runs on every tap.
- **The cookie is signed with `FELLOWS_SHEET_SECRET`**, reused rather than adding a variable to set on a deadline, via a key derived from it (`centre-lead-session-v1`) so the cookie is never signed with the raw bearer token. Two consequences, both accepted: that token is also POSTed to the Fellows sheet's Apps Script, so anyone who can open that script project can mint a lead session; and rotating it signs every centre lead out. Neither is the weak link while an email alone is the credential.
- **One sitting is open at a time**, enforced by a partial unique index on `paper_exams (edition_year) where attendance_open`. That flag is also the off switch: with nothing open, no lead can sign in or mark.
- **The event log is not the register.** A failure to append an event is logged and swallowed rather than rolling back the mark, because the hall depends on the register and not on its history.
- **Not modelled:** offline queueing (a lead with no signal sees failed rows and re-taps), QR or barcode identification, lateness or arrival time beyond `marked_at`, and per-session attendance for an exam sat in more than one sitting.
